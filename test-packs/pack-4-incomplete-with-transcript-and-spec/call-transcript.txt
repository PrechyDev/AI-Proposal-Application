Discovery call - Larkspur Events Co.
Date: 2026-09-07
Attendees: Naomi Fischer (Operations Lead, Larkspur Events Co.), Salesperson (Koya Talent)

[00:00]
Salesperson: Thanks for making time, Naomi. Walk me through what's going on at Larkspur right now.

Naomi: Sure. We run corporate events - conferences, product launches, that kind of thing - and for every event we're coordinating somewhere between 15 and 25 vendors: catering, AV, staffing agencies, florists, the works. Right now all of that lives in one massive shared spreadsheet per event, and it's a nightmare to keep current.

[02:30]
Salesperson: What specifically goes wrong with the spreadsheet approach?

Naomi: The biggest issue is confirmation tracking. We send each vendor an email asking them to confirm their booking, deliverables, and arrival time, and then someone on my team has to manually check who's actually replied and update the sheet by hand. On our last event, two vendors never got a follow-up because it fell through the cracks, and one of them was the AV company - we found out three days before the event that they hadn't confirmed at all.

[05:10]
Salesperson: How big a team are we talking about handling this?

Naomi: Just three of us, and we run about six to eight events a month during peak season. Each event has its own spreadsheet, so there's no single place to see "which vendors across all our upcoming events haven't confirmed yet."

[07:45]
Salesperson: What would actually solve this for you?

Naomi: Something that automatically sends the confirmation request, tracks who's replied and who hasn't, and flags anything that's gone quiet after a few days - so nobody finds out three days out that a vendor never confirmed. Ideally one dashboard across all our upcoming events, not one spreadsheet per event.

[10:20]
Salesperson: Do vendors typically confirm by email, or do some use other channels?

Naomi: Almost entirely email. A few of our long-term vendors text one of us directly, but that's the exception, and honestly we'd rather get everyone into one consistent process anyway.

[12:00]
Salesperson: Any timing pressure here?

Naomi: Our next peak season starts in about eight weeks, so getting this in place before then would let us test it on a slightly quieter month first rather than throwing it straight into the busiest stretch.

[13:15]
Salesperson: Got it, I'll put a proposal together based on this.

Naomi: Perfect, thank you.
