Discovery call - Harborline Freight Co.
Date: 2026-09-08
Attendees: Marcus Webb (Operations Director, Harborline Freight Co.), Salesperson (Koya Talent)

[00:00]
Salesperson: Thanks for jumping on, Marcus. Tell me a bit about what's going on at Harborline right now.

Marcus: Sure. So we handle freight forwarding for mostly mid-size importers, and a huge part of our week is customs paperwork. Every shipment needs a commercial invoice, a packing list, and a customs declaration, and right now our team fills those out by hand for every single shipment, cross-referencing three different supplier documents each time.

[02:15]
Salesperson: How many shipments a week are we talking about?

Marcus: Somewhere between 60 and 90, depending on the week. And it's not just volume - the actual problem is errors. If a customs declaration doesn't match the commercial invoice exactly, down to line-item totals, the shipment gets held at the port for review. That's happened to us six times in the last two months alone, and each hold costs the client money and costs us the relationship.

[05:40]
Salesperson: Got it. So what would actually help here?

Marcus: Honestly, if the customs paperwork could just be generated automatically from the supplier documents - pull the line items, match totals, flag anything that doesn't reconcile before it ever gets submitted - that would save us probably 15-20 hours a week across the team, and more importantly it'd stop the holds.

[08:20]
Salesperson: Do your supplier documents come in a consistent format?

Marcus: Not really, that's part of the challenge. Some suppliers send PDFs, some send Excel sheets, a couple of the smaller ones still email us plain text. Whatever gets built needs to handle that, because we can't force our suppliers to change how they send things.

[11:05]
Salesperson: Understood. Any timeline pressure on your end?

Marcus: We've got a busier import season starting in about six weeks, so ideally something's live before then. And obviously the goal is cutting down on those customs holds - if we can get through that busy season without a repeat of the last two months, that alone justifies this for us.

[13:30]
Salesperson: Makes sense. I'll put together a proposal based on this and get it over to you.

Marcus: Sounds good, appreciate it.
